<p>Ronald Read was a janitor and gas station attendant who quietly amassed an US$8 million fortune by the time of his death. He achieved this not by earning a high income but by living frugally and consistently investing in the stock market over many years. His story is a powerful example of how even with a modest income, investing in the stock market can yield significant returns over time.</p><p>Part 1 of the project tests the truths behind Ronald Read’s story. Through this analysis, we will explore whether long-term investing in a diversified index fund like SPY can indeed yield substantial returns, even for those with limited financial resources.</p><h3>Assumptions</h3><p>Before proceeding with the analysis, we make several assumptions that are designed to reflect a realistic yet simplified scenario for a typical retail investor with no advanced investing knowledge. The goal is to demonstrate that, by simply buying and holding an index fund like SPY, an investor can significantly increase their net worth over time compared to leaving the same amount of money in a savings account.</p><p>The scope for analysis goes from January 2000 to December 2024.</p><ol><li>The portfolio only consists of SPY.</li><li>The investor is patient and disciplined, meaning they will not panic sell during market downturns. <strong>They will hold their investment in SPY for the full 24-year period, regardless of short-term market volatility.</strong></li><li>The investor has <strong>no debts</strong> to pay. They also have <strong>no unexpected expenses</strong> that require them to liquidate their existing investments.</li><li>The investor incurs <strong>no brokerage fees</strong> when purchasing SPY.</li><li>No taxes on the purchase and ownership of shares.</li><li>Dividends are <strong>taken as cash and not reinvested</strong>.</li><li><strong>The interest rate</strong> on savings accounts for banks <strong>are constant</strong> throughout the 20-year period. While interest rates can fluctuate, this assumption simplifies the comparison by focusing on the long-term outcomes of each option. We take the value of <strong>0.0125% per month for a high interest savings account</strong>.</li><li>Contributions to the investor’s portfolio are constant unless stated otherwise.</li><li>No inflation. This assumption will be removed in Part 2.</li></ol><h3>Historical Trends and Performance of SPY from 2000 to 2024</h3><p>We analyse the movements of the price of SPY before discussing the potential returns and any comparisons.</p><p>First we look at the price of SPY from its inception to December 2024.</p><figure><img alt="" src="https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/1024/1*KUjZUqbwu7hG4s3q3lZsPA.png" /><figcaption>Figure 1: Overall SPY trend from Jan 2000 — Dec 2024</figcaption></figure><p>If you had purchased SPY in 2000, you would have faced losses from 2000 to 2003. Although the market recovered by 2007, it crashed again in 2008. It wasn’t until 2010 that SPY returned to its 2000 price. While the market has generally trended upwards since then, there have still been several periods of significant negative fluctuations.</p><p>It would be valuable to analyse the geopolitical and macroeconomic factors that impacted SPY’s price over the 24-year period. While we cannot cover every event, this analysis will focus on the major ones that had the most significant influence.</p><p>We break Figure 1 into 4 separate plots, each analysing various time periods where there have been significant changes in the markets.</p><figure><img alt="" src="https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/1024/1*TU2474N_GTtf3C-EnByR0w.png" /><figcaption>Figure 2: The plots show SPY’s adjusted closing prices from 2000 to 2024, divided into four periods. Each subplot highlights key events.</figcaption></figure><p><strong>2000–2003</strong></p><ul><li>March 2000: Dot-Com bubble burst — Overvalued tech stocks collapsed</li><li>September 2001: 9/11 Attacks — Fear and uncertainty following the terrorist attacks</li><li>December 2001 and July 2002 : Corporate Fraud cases of Enron and Worldcom — Continued market instability</li><li>March 2003 : U.S. invades Iraq again — Initial uncertainty, markets recovered</li></ul><p><strong>2004–2008 — </strong>Financial Crisis of 2008. U.S. housing bubble collapsed.</p><ul><li>Banks had issued risky subprime mortgages to people with poor credit, and these loans were bundled into mortgage-backed securities and sold to investors. When housing prices fell, many homeowners defaulted on their loans, causing the value of mortgage-backed securities to plummet.</li><li>As a result, major banks like Lehman Brothers collapsed. The crisis spread globally, causing a significant drop in stock markets, including SPY.</li></ul><p><strong>2009–2015</strong></p><p>U.S. debt ceiling crisis of 2011.</p><ul><li>The U.S. debt ceiling is a legal limit set by Congress on the total amount of money that the federal government is allowed to borrow to meet its financial obligations.</li><li>In 2011, the U.S. approached its debt ceiling, leading to a major political standoff in Congress. There was intense debate over whether to raise the ceiling, with concerns about the growing national debt. Failure to raise the debt ceiling would have meant the U.S. government could default on its obligations, which would have had severe economic consequences.</li><li>The uncertainty during this period caused significant volatility in the stock markets. Eventually, a last-minute deal was reached to raise the ceiling, but the crisis led to the first-ever downgrade of the U.S. credit rating by Standard &amp; Poor’s, which further shook investor confidence and caused markets to drop.</li></ul><p><strong>2015–2024</strong></p><ul><li>February 2020: COVID-19 — Global lock-downs due to the spread of the virus, severe slowdown in economic activity leading to a decline in economic activity and the collapse of the S&amp;P 500.<br>Massive government stimulus packages, central bank interventions, and development of a vaccine eventually led to a market recovery.</li><li>February 2022 : Russian invasion of Ukraine -Russia was sanctioned following the invasion, it created uncertainty in global markets, especially due to concerns over disruptions in energy supplies, as Russia is a major exporter of oil and natural gas. While markets eventually stabilised, the invasion highlighted the sensitivity of global markets to geopolitical events.</li></ul><h3>Analysis on Nominal Returns — Lump Sum</h3><p>In this section, we compare two different investment strategies: a one-time investment of $10,000 in SPY, left untouched over the years, versus regularly investing smaller amounts into SPY over time. We’ll also compare these strategies against the returns from keeping $10,000 in a high-yield savings account.</p><p>Firstly, we look at Investing $10,000 only in SPY in 2000. Suppose you are a university student and you invest US$10,000 into SPY.</p><figure><img alt="" src="https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/1024/1*vj2iBxj79p2dJ4YwChrbLw.png" /><figcaption>Figure 3: How the value of a portfolio of US$ 10,000 worth of SPY invested in 2000 changes over time</figcaption></figure><p>Figure 3 shows that a $10,000 investment in SPY in 2000 would have grown to $65,355.91 by August 2024. This represents a more than 6x return on investment.</p><p>Now we compare it to leaving $10,000 in a savings account in 2000 that yields interest rate of 1.5% per annum.</p><figure><img alt="" src="https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/981/1*lz0RKBQrQifsHArjMl_mFQ.png" /><figcaption>Figure 4: How US$ 10,000 in a savings account that compounds monthly by 1.5% deposited in 2000 changes over time</figcaption></figure><figure><img alt="" src="https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/1*I6pzrG56dijRsksXKglXoA.png" /><figcaption>Figure 5 : Comparison between the baseline of $10,000 vs the other 2 options</figcaption></figure><p>The same $10,000 investment in a savings account would have grown to about $14,546.51 over the same period. While this growth is steady and reliable, it pales in comparison to the returns generated by SPY.</p><h3>Analysis on Nominal Returns — Lump Sum &amp; DCA</h3><p>Now, we consider the case when the investor consistently purchases fixed amount of the security every month. (Dollar Cost Averaging — DCA)</p><p>Looking at the same university student in the previous section. We assume they just got a job that provides an monthly salary of 4000 USD (after taxes and other deductibles). They invest in the stock market with DCA in the following manner:</p><figure><img alt="" src="https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/684/1*ytMquL8jKr0fH1VJuqshuQ.png" /></figure><p>The rationale for such increments are as follows.</p><ul><li>The investor gets a promotion every 2 years on their job that increases their salary by US$500.</li><li>They continue to live below their means and put the extra money into SPY.</li><li>After 2006, the investor starts a family with 2 children and purchases big ticket items like housing and a vehicle for the family.</li><li>They cannot afford to put in more than US$ 2000 monthly.</li><li>Doing some basic calculations, the investor invested an average of US$ 1750 monthly from 2000–2024.</li></ul><p>Now let’s visualise the returns from dollar cost averaging for 24 years.</p><figure><img alt="" src="https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/1024/1*va87jpt62wyjJW2pFRYcOw.png" /><figcaption>Figure 6: Portfolio Value in 2024 after an initial US$ 10,000 and a constant DCA amount every month.</figcaption></figure><p>The returns from consistent increment DCA is extremely significant. It turned US$10,000 into nearly US$2,500,000 in 24 years.</p><p>They invested US$541,200 in total. This would result in a more than 4x return on investment.</p><p>Compare this with depositing the same amount in a high yield savings account for 24 years.</p><figure><img alt="" src="https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/980/1*hVxDhleyFWFXgTZhehykqw.png" /><figcaption>Figure 7: Value of savings in the account after 24 years after an initial US$ 10,000 and a constant deposit every month.</figcaption></figure><p>It appears that consistently investing the same amount of money in SPY will yield significantly more money than leaving it in a savings account.</p><p>We also look at the minimum amount to reach US$ 1,000,000 with SPY. You need to invest US$678 per month to reach 1 million USD from 2000–2024.</p><figure><img alt="" src="https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/1024/1*Yj2sfnefcnAO0YvZV7XIGA.png" /><figcaption>Figure 8 : Visualising the minimum value you will need to hit 1 million USD from 2020–2024</figcaption></figure><p>Now suppose you can only contribute US$500 every month.</p><figure><img alt="" src="https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/1024/1*i7uHiUytHOSXpz1TzsckKw.png" /><figcaption>Figure 9 : Visualising the returns of investing US$500 into SPY ever month</figcaption></figure><figure><img alt="" src="https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/864/1*F0oya_l7-kBRIDas40wOvg.png" /><figcaption>Figure 10 : Comparison between each investment and savings approach</figcaption></figure><p>The results show clear differences in final outcomes:</p><ol><li><strong>Savings Account</strong>: Despite being the safest option among the options presented, the savings account yielded the lowest return of $640,887 over the period.</li><li><strong>$500 DCA</strong>: A steady $500 monthly contribution led to a final value of US$814,712.</li><li><strong>$678 DCA</strong>: Adjusting the monthly contribution to US$678 allowed the portfolio to reach US$1,000,000.</li><li><strong>Increments DCA</strong>: The most aggressive (and successful) strategy, involving incremental increases in contributions with an average contribution of US$ 1750 per month, resulted in a substantial final value of US$2,485,796. This approach maximised the benefits of compounding and market growth, outperforming all other strategies.</li></ol><p>Now let’s compare my analyses to the ground truths.</p><p><a href="https://www.cbsnews.com/news/retirement-savings-how-much-americans-need-1-46-million/">According to a recent CBS news report</a>, Americans feel they need US$ 1.46 million to comfortably retire. Leaving your savings in the high interest account cannot get you there.</p><p>Investing a significant amount of money is crucial for substantial compounding. By using the DCA strategy with regular increments, you could be in a strong position to retire after 24 years, assuming all other factors remain unchanged.</p><p>Let’s not forget that SPY pays dividends, the investor could potentially live off their dividends too.</p><h3>Key Insights</h3><p>Throughout the project, we have derived key insights.</p><ul><li>You will not save enough to comfortably retire solely relying on interest rates from saving accounts.</li><li>The investor does not need any investing knowledge, they just need to open a brokerage account and purchase SPY.</li><li>The investor must be patient. They may not see returns in the short runs, but they will definitely see returns in the long run.</li><li>The investor must on average, contribute US$1000 monthly in order to see returns that allow them to retire.</li></ul><h3>Conclusion</h3><p>The analysis of investing in the SPY reinforces the key lesson from Ronald Read’s story: consistent, long-term investing in stocks can yield significant returns. With more contributions, the power of compounding and the overall growth of the market can lead to substantial wealth accumulation over time.</p><p>Just as Ronald Read quietly built his fortune, <strong>anyone can harness these same principles to grow their wealth</strong>, proving that investing in the stock market is a powerful tool for financial growth.</p><p><a href="/blog/spy-analytics-forecasting-part-2-hedging-against-inflation">In the next part</a>, I will go into a deep dive into inflation’s role in investments.</p><p>Check out the project’s <a href="https://github.com/dhcchh/SPY-Analytics-Forecasting-Project">GitHub repository</a> if you are interested.</p>
